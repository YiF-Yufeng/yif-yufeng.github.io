About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Earth System Science at Tsinghua University, advised by Prof. Yanluan Lin. My research focuses on identifying the physical constraints that govern tropical cyclone frequency and understanding its response to climate change. Beyond this, I am broadly interested in multiscale climate dynamics, extreme weather, idealized modeling, and complex-system perspectives on the Earth system.

I enjoy identifying and discovering emerging patterns in complex climate systems, and proposing novel mathematical and physical approaches to address fundamental questions in atmospheric and climate sciences.

Research Highlights

A Spatiotemporal Packing Framework for Tropical Cyclone Frequency Dissertation

Proposes a framework showing that global tropical cyclone frequency emerges from the spatiotemporal packing and self-organization of the tropical climate system under finite environmental constraints — linking spatial convective organization, genesis interval statistics, and TC lifecycle effects.

Information-Theoretic Quantification of Tropical Convective Organization

Develops an entropy-based Hierarchical Organization Index (HOI) and a dual-metric framework integrating size hierarchy and spatial clustering. Identifies an adaptive structure-optimal infrared brightness temperature threshold and its long-term cooling trend, revealing systematic structural changes in tropical convection.

Climate-Driven Changes in Tropical Cyclone Characteristics

Reveals changes in TC intensity, air-sea interaction, and motion variability by decomposing lifetime maximum intensity into intensification rate and duration, quantifying the weakening of TC-induced ocean cooling, and identifying increasing track curvature.
